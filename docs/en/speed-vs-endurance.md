# Speed vs Endurance Racing Pigeons: The Science of Gene Combination Breeding

> **Key takeaway**: Behind the speed type and the endurance type are two entirely different physiological routes — anaerobic burst fuelling and aerobic sustained fuelling. The difference between them is determined by combinations of flight genes such as LDHA, MSTN, CRY1 and DRD4. This article covers fuelling mechanisms, muscle fibre types, key genes and combination strategy to help fanciers breed scientifically by race distance.

Racing pigeon events are usually divided by distance into the short course (200–400 km), the middle distance (400–700 km) and the long course (over 700 km). Different distances make entirely different demands on a racing pigeon's **speed** and **endurance**, and behind those two capacities are different **gene combinations** at work. Traditionally, fanciers relied on pedigrees and race results to judge how fast and how far a pigeon could fly; today, [racing pigeon flight ability genetic testing](https://sanshibio.net/flight-ability/) moves "selecting for distance" from experience towards science, and gives objective grounds for breeding speed-type and endurance-type racing pigeons.

## Speed and endurance: two physiological routes

The essential difference between the speed type and the endurance type lies in how the muscle fuels itself and contracts:

| Dimension | Speed type (short course) | Endurance type (long course) |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Fuelling mode | mainly anaerobic glycolysis | mainly aerobic oxidation |
| Muscle fibre type | high proportion of fast-twitch fibres | high proportion of slow-twitch fibres |
| Key genes | MSTN, F-KER and others | LDHA, CRY1 and others |
| Physiological traits | strong burst power, lactate tolerance | strong staying power, resistance to fatigue |
| Typical events | 200–400 km sprints | long hauls over 700 km |

Put simply, the speed-type racing pigeon "bursts in an instant and sprints briefly", while the endurance type "delivers continuously and stays the course over long distances". The great majority of racing pigeons sit somewhere between the two, differing only in tendency — there is no absolute dividing line.

## Molecular mechanism: why genes determine speed and endurance

The difference between speed and endurance comes down to two molecular levels, **energy metabolism** and **muscle fibre type**.

**Fuelling metabolism**: during a short-course sprint, oxygen supply to the muscle cannot keep up and the cell switches to **anaerobic glycolysis** for rapid energy production, with lactate as a by-product; lactate accumulation acidifies the muscle and causes fatigue. Long-distance flight instead depends on **aerobic oxidation**, steadily breaking down sugars and fats — slower to produce energy, but lasting. The key enzyme regulating this process is [lactate dehydrogenase LDHA](ldha-endurance.md) — individuals with a favourable LDHA genotype clear lactate faster and resist fatigue better, which is the core molecular basis of the endurance marker.

**Muscle fibre type**: the pigeon breast muscle is built from a mix of fast-twitch and slow-twitch fibres. Fast-twitch fibres contract quickly and generate more force but tire easily, while slow-twitch fibres contract slowly and resist fatigue. [Myostatin MSTN](mstn-muscle.md) is a negative regulator of muscle growth, and its genotype affects how well the breast muscle develops and the proportion of fast-twitch fibres, directly determining burst power and sprint speed.

In addition, the tightness of the feather vanes determined by the [feather gene F-KER](f-ker-feather.md) affects flight drag, while the [navigation gene CRY1](cry1-navigation.md) and the [homing gene DRD4](drd4-homing.md) determine whether the bird can come home accurately and resolutely over a long course. Speed and endurance are not the product of a single gene but the result of many genes working together, which is why this knowledge base stresses "combination breeding".

## Key genes explained

| Gene | Chinese name | Role in speed / endurance |
|------|--------|---------------------|
| MSTN | 肌肉生长抑素 | burst power and sprint speed |
| F-KER | 羽角蛋白 | feather aerodynamic performance |
| LDHA | 乳酸脱氢酶 A | endurance and lactate clearance |
| CRY1 | 隐花色素 1 | long-distance navigation and orientation |
| DRD4 | 多巴胺受体 D4 | homing persistence and consistency |

The core of a short-course speed race is "muscle + feather", and the core of a long-course endurance race is "endurance + navigation + homing". This is the fundamental reason why breeding priorities differ between the speed type and the endurance type.

## Combination breeding strategy

A single gene can hardly determine results; **multi-gene combinations** are the basis of scientific breeding:

| Event type | Recommended gene combination |
|----------|------------------|
| Short-course speed race | MSTN + F-KER (burst power + feather structure) |
| Middle-distance race | MSTN + LDHA + DRD4 (balanced speed and endurance) |
| Long-course endurance race | LDHA + CRY1 + DRD4 (endurance + navigation + homing) |

## Practical breeding steps

1. **Build the gene file first**: run [flight ability genetic testing](https://sanshibio.net/flight-ability/) on your core breeders to establish each genotype
2. **Pair to the target**: choose complementary gene combinations to pair according to race distance (for the short course, for example, a MSTN-strength individual × a F-KER-strength individual)
3. **Verify with phenotypes**: the genotype is the "potential" and race results are the "performance"; assess the two together and avoid gene-only determinism
4. **Keep recording and tracking**: build the correspondence between the offspring's race results and their genotypes, and the breeding model can be optimised after one or two generations

> 💡 Tip: genetic testing provides a scientific probability reference, and breeding still has to take pedigree, constitution and training into account. For the full methodology see [combination breeding strategy](breeding-strategy.md).

## Key points

1. The speed type and the endurance type are two physiological routes — anaerobic burst vs aerobic persistence.
2. The short course looks at MSTN + F-KER — burst power and feather structure determine sprint speed.
3. The long course looks at LDHA + CRY1 + DRD4 — endurance + navigation + homing, none dispensable.
4. The middle distance needs balance — an even combination of speed and endurance genes.
5. Build the gene file before pairing — choose complementary gene combinations for the race distance.

## FAQ

### Can genetic testing predict results?

It cannot directly predict placings. Genetic testing assesses a racing pigeon's **genetic potential** in dimensions such as speed, endurance and navigation and gives direction to pairing and training; actual results are also shaped by training, weather and condition.

### Can a speed-type pigeon never fly a long distance?

Not necessarily. The genotype reflects a "tendency", not an "absolute limit". A speed-type pigeon can show its advantage over a suitable distance, while a long distance needs stronger endurance genes behind it; assess it together with [combination breeding strategy](breeding-strategy.md).

### How do I tell whether my racing pigeon is the speed type or the endurance type?

Use [flight ability genetic testing](https://sanshibio.net/flight-ability/) to look at key genotypes such as MSTN and LDHA, then judge together with the pedigree and race results. Genetic testing provides objective genetic grounds.

### Is testing just one or two genes enough?

No. Flight ability is the result of many genes working together, so testing the complete eight-marker panel is recommended; only a combined assessment supports scientific breeding and avoids drawing conclusions from a part.

### Can a pigeon with both speed and endurance be bred?

Balance is worth pursuing, but there is a physiological trade-off between speed and endurance. Middle-distance events (400–700 km) are the better fit for breeding balanced racing pigeons that carry both speed and endurance genes.

### Can genetic testing give pairing advice?

The test report gives breeding advice based on each genotype, helping fanciers design pairing plans for the target race distance and avoid blind matings.
