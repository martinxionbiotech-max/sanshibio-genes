# Racing Pigeon Gene Report: From Structure to Pairing

> **Key takeaway**: The heart of reading a racing pigeon gene test report is separating three layers — genotype, ability indicator and breeding advice. A report is made up of five sections: sample information, test items, test results, indicator interpretation and conclusion and advice. Each of the 8 flight-ability indicators corresponds to one genotype, and what they reflect is genetic potential rather than final results. The key to reading the report is translating genotypes into a breeding direction, then using the report's conclusions to guide pairing decisions.

Many fanciers open a racing pigeon flight-ability gene test report and their first reaction is that they cannot make sense of it — gene abbreviations, genotype symbols and indicator ratings everywhere. In fact, once you master the three-step method of looking at the structure first, the indicators second and the advice last, a report turns from an unreadable sheet of paper into a breeding tool you can actually use. This article breaks down the whole process of reading a gene test report.

## Basic report structure: five sections

A complete Sanshi Bio flight-ability gene test report normally consists of five sections, and should be read from top to bottom:

| Report section | Content | Reading points |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Sample information | Pigeon ID, leg band number, sex, sampling date | Check first that the sample matches the bird, to avoid a mix-up |
| Test items | Flight-ability genes / pathogens / parentage, etc. | Confirm which type of test was ordered |
| Test results | The genotype at each gene locus (e.g. homozygous/heterozygous) | This is the raw data, and the basis for everything that follows |
| Indicator interpretation | Assessment of ability dimensions such as endurance, homing and navigation | Translates genotypes into ability tendencies (good / average / watch) |
| Conclusion and advice | Overall breeding direction and pairing suggestions | Where the report's core value lies — it guides practice directly |

It is worth stressing that the conclusion and advice section is a composite assessment and is more useful than looking at a single genotype in isolation. When reading, do not fixate on any one good or average verdict — look at the overall picture.

## The 8 flight-ability indicators

A flight-ability report gives results for 8 indicators, each corresponding to one genotype. Knowing what each indicator governs is the prerequisite for reading the report:

| Indicator | Gene | Gene function | Reading points |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| Endurance | LDHA | Lactate dehydrogenase A; lactate metabolism | Fatigue resistance and staying power on long flights |
| Homing | DRD4 | Dopamine receptor D4 | Persistence and consistency in homing |
| Navigation | CRY1 | Cryptochrome 1 | Accuracy of long-distance orientation |
| Muscle strength | MSTN | Myostatin | Muscle strength and burst sprinting |
| Feather | F-KER | Feather keratin | Feather quality and flight drag |
| Memory | LRP8 | LDL receptor related protein 8 | Learning, memory and training response |
| Rainy-day navigation | GSR | Glutathione reductase | Orientation in bad weather |
| Cognition | CASK | Calcium/calmodulin-dependent serine protein kinase | Cognitive trainability |

**Core reading principle**: a genotype reflects genetic potential, not the final result. Within the same pigeon the 8 indicators are usually a mix of strengths and weaknesses — for example LDHA good but DRD4 average means the bird can keep flying but may lack homing persistence. Judge it against the intended race format rather than looking at one item alone. For the molecular mechanisms behind each indicator see the dedicated articles on the [endurance gene LDHA](ldha-endurance.md), the [homing gene DRD4](drd4-homing.md), the [navigation gene CRY1](cry1-navigation.md) and others.

## Molecular mechanism: how genotypes produce the indicator readings

Behind the dry-looking genotype symbols on a report lies a causal chain of gene → protein → function → ability. Take the endurance indicator LDHA as an example: the LDHA gene encodes lactate dehydrogenase A, the rate-limiting enzyme at the end of glycolysis, catalysing pyruvate + NADH → lactate + NAD⁺ so that muscle can keep supplying energy when oxygen is short or flight intensity is high. Different genotypes affect the enzyme's expression level or activity, and hence the speed of lactate clearance — this is the molecular basis for LDHA good genotype = stronger endurance.

Likewise, DRD4 encodes a dopamine receptor that acts on reward and motivation circuits and determines whether a pigeon wants to come home; CRY1 takes part in light perception and geomagnetic sensing and determines whether it can find home; MSTN is a negative regulator of muscle growth that determines breast-muscle development and burst power. Once you understand this chain, it is clear why the report translates genotypes into ability indicators rather than giving a placing prediction. This is the concrete expression of the rule that genotype determines phenotypic potential, set out in [pigeon genetics basics](pigeon-genetics-basics.md).

## From genotype to breeding advice: a four-step method

To turn the report into action, work through four steps:

1. **Identify the strengths**: circle the good items among the 8 to pin down this bird's genetic strong points (for example endurance or navigation).
2. **Identify the weak points**: circle the watch/average items to pin down the genetic weaknesses (for example homing or feather).
3. **Match the race format**: short-distance sprints favour MSTN + F-KER; long-distance endurance favours LDHA + CRY1 + DRD4. See [speed vs endurance gene breeding](speed-vs-endurance.md).
4. **Design the pairing**: let strengths and weaknesses complement each other — one parent strong in endurance, the other strong in homing, so the offspring is more likely to combine both.

> 💡 Tip: the report's conclusion is directional advice, not a pairing order. Putting it into practice still requires weighing pedigree, race results, physical condition and training.

## How the report guides pairing: from reading results to making decisions

The greatest value of a gene test report is that it turns pairing — traditionally a job for experience — into a decision with evidence behind it:

| Pairing goal | What the report suggests | Suggested action |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Reinforce a strength | One parent is good on that indicator, the other average | Pair two good types to raise the odds of passing it on to the offspring |
| Make up a weakness | Both parents are average on that indicator | Bring in an outcross that is good on that indicator, then back-cross |
| Overall balance | Each parent is strong in a different area | Complementary pairing, aiming for balance across several indicators |

Before pairing, it is worth building a long-term [genetic ID](gene-id-card.md) archive for your core breeders, and using [parentage testing](https://sanshibio.net/paternity/) to confirm the bloodline, completing the breeding loop of genetic file + bloodline + race results. For the full methodology see [breeding strategy](breeding-strategy.md).

## FAQ

### How do I read a gene report without falling into traps?

Remember three things. First, check that the sample information matches the bird. Second, treat the 8 indicators as one overall picture rather than staring at a single item. Third, genotypes reflect potential, not results — do not read good as a guaranteed placing.

### What does genotype mean on a gene report?

It means the combination of alleles the pigeon carries at that locus (homozygous or heterozygous). On that basis the report gives an assessment of the corresponding ability; what matters is the three-level verdict of good, average or watch, not the genotype symbols themselves.

### If the genotype is good but race results are poor, is the report wrong?

The report reflects genetic potential; race results are influenced by training, nutrition, weather, disease, form on the day and many other factors. The two do not contradict each other — good potential is not the same as good performance. The report is a breeding tool, not a results predictor.

### With the 8 indicators, is more items rated good always better?

Not necessarily. What matters is matching the race format. Short-distance sprints value MSTN and F-KER; long distance values LDHA, CRY1 and DRD4. Overall balance, with no fatal weak point, is more practical than simply piling up good ratings.

### Can the report give pairing advice directly?

Yes. The conclusion and advice section combines the genotypes to give a breeding direction, helping fanciers design a pairing plan for their target race format — though in practice it still has to be weighed against pedigree and race results.
